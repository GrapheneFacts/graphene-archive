## Scientific & Technical Developments

### 1. Graphene stencil for placing molecules on gold (nanoelectronics / biosensors)
**Summary:** UCLA (Paul Weiss et al.) used a graphene sheet with tiny holes as a mask on gold so molecules attach only where intended. Goal: patterned nanoelectronic devices and potential brain-signal sensors. Published in *ACS Nano*.  
**Sources:** [UCLA Health release](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/release/ucla-scientists-create-graphene-barrier-to-precisely-control-molecules-for-making-nanoelectronics) (14–15 Sep 2026); *ACS Nano*.  
**Evidence:** Well-supported  
**Post draft:** UCLA team used a holed graphene sheet as a molecular stencil on gold, placing molecules only where needed for nanoscale devices and possible biosensors. Paper: ACS Nano.

### 2. Water dissociation at graphene vacancies (computational)
**Summary:** Machine-learned potentials show solvation changes how water breaks at a graphene single vacancy: competing acidic and basic paths, not the gas-phase concerted path. Implications for functionalization and nanofluidics. arXiv 2609.11602, submitted 10 Sep 2026.  
**Sources:** [arXiv:2609.11602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11602)  
**Evidence:** Preliminary (preprint, simulation)  
**Post draft:** New preprint: solvated water at a graphene vacancy splits via two competing paths (not the gas-phase route). Defect chemistry + wettability may matter for membranes and nanofluidics.

### 3. Graphene fibres via viscous flow of graphene oxide
**Summary:** *Nature Materials* news & views (8 Sep) on polymer-like viscoelasticity of GO in glycerol enabling ultrahigh draw ratios in wet spinning; fibres with strong mechanical, thermal, and electrical properties.  
**Sources:** [Nature Materials](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-026-02735-y)  
**Evidence:** Well-supported  
**Post draft:** GO sheets in viscous solvent can be drawn like a polymer. Result: graphene fibres combining strength, thermal conductivity, and electrical conductivity. Nature Materials.

### 4. Graphene zone-plate optical PUF (hardware authentication)
**Summary:** Work using graphene diffraction zone plates + RGB light challenges + a transformer model to turn wavelength-dependent patterns into compact binary responses. Paper in *Opto-Electronic Advances* (online July; press 14 Sep).  
**Sources:** EIN Presswire coverage, 14 Sep 2026.  
**Evidence:** Well-supported (published optics paper; press is promotional)  
**Post draft:** Graphene zone plates plus multi-colour light challenges can act as a physical unclonable function for hardware authentication.

### 5. Graphene oxide dew-point / icing sensor (patent)
**Summary:** Stony Brook / SUNY patented a GO-film method using adsorption and the water–ice transition plus proton conduction to estimate dew point; possible cheaper alternative to chilled-mirror hygrometers; aviation icing interest. Licensing via RF SUNY.

**Sources:** [GMG announcement](https://graphenemg.com/gmg-announces-long-life-battery-cycling-data/)

## Regulatory / Policy

### 15. NIOSH workplace graphene exposure measurements
**Summary:** NIOSH sampled 44 workers at 11 US graphene producers/users. Against a conservative occupational exposure band of **10 µg/m³**, many samples exceeded it when dry powder was handled. Recommendation: supply graphene in masterbatch/liquid rather than loose powder. No reported human disease cluster tied to these data.  
**Sources:** CompositesWorld; IOM3; NIOSH/JOEH banding work; Advanced Carbons Council webinar materials.  
**Evidence:** Well-supported occupational hygiene data; 10 µg/m³ is a **band**, not a formal OSHA PEL  
**Post draft:** NIOSH measured graphene-family exposures at 11 US sites. Many samples beat a conservative 10 µg/m³ band when dry powder is handled. Advice: keep graphene in masterbatch or liquid, not loose dust.
